# @iirc/web

The web frontend: a React 19 view over [`@iirc/frontend`](../../packages/frontend/README.md), its pure core ([ADR 0010](../../docs/adr/0010-the-web-frontend-is-a-pure-core-under-react.md)), built with Vite. It is a Frontend like any other, and reaches the Daemon only through [`@iirc/api`](../../packages/api/README.md), which the lint fence enforces. It is served on the API's own origin, by `iirc daemon --web` ([ADR 0007](../../docs/adr/0007-one-binary-shapes-by-argument.md)).

```sh
bun run build:web                                        # at the root: apps/web/dist
bun run iirc daemon --data-dir ~/.local/share/iirc --web=apps/web/dist
```

## What it does

- **Signs in with a key.** On load it asks whether the browser is signed in, with one authorized read, `GET /api/networks`. Signed out, it asks for a key and gives it to `POST /api/sign-in` once, for a sign-in the browser holds in an `HttpOnly` cookie. Why a sign-in failed is said in words: a wrong key, a request from another origin, the Daemon unable to save its sign-ins, or the Daemon not answering, with a way to try again.
- **Shows the Networks and their Timelines** in a sidebar: a tree of each Network, by id, with how its connection stands, and under it its own Timeline, then its Channels, then its Direct Messages, each by name. Arrows move and expand, Enter or Space opens, typing a name finds it, and choosing a Timeline opens it in the focused Pane, or focuses the Pane it is open in. It marks only what the focused Pane shows, the core's `chosenOf`: Options while the options show, the Timeline while it shows, and nothing for the search or a Direct Message not yet begun, so a Timeline open behind the options is not marked, and choosing it shows it.
- **Shows what is new, calmly.** Each sidebar row's name is coloured by the highest level unread in it, from the Daemon's counts: read and Activity dim, Messages as text, Direct Messages and Highlights accented and heavier. Beside it, small numbers: Highlights and Direct Messages accented, other Messages dim, and `999+` past what the Daemon counts. A Network's row sums its Timelines and shows only what calls for the Owner, Highlights and Direct Messages. A row's label says it for a screen reader, such as `#iirc, 3 unread, 1 highlight`, and a Network's adds how its connection stands. The page's title counts the Highlights and Direct Messages unread across every Network, `(3) iirc`. A Highlight in a Timeline is tinted, with a mark at its edge. A Timeline opened with something unread shows a "new" divider before the first item after the Read Marker it opened with, which stays while it stays open, whatever the Daemon's marker does.
- **Splits into Panes,** as a tiling window manager does, dense on a desktop. `/split`, or Alt+Shift+S, splits the focused Pane side by side, and `/split v`, or Alt+Shift+D, one above another, the new Pane opening what calls for the Owner most that no Pane shows, or nothing. Alt+Shift and the arrows, or `/pane left`, move to the Pane beside, as the screen lies, `/swap` swaps with it, Alt+Shift+W or `/close` closes the focused Pane, the one beside taking its place, Alt+Shift+Z or `/zoom` shows it alone and back, Alt+Shift+E or `/equalize` evens every split, and Alt+Shift+G and Alt+Shift+R, or `/grow` and `/shrink`, size it by a step. A divider drags, takes the arrow keys once focused, and gives equal shares on a double click. A press anywhere in a Pane, or the focus moving into it, focuses it. Each Pane has its own header, with its number while there are several, its own Timeline, where it was scrolled, and its own members beside it, and the focused one an accented edge along its top. A Timeline is open in one Pane at most: opening it again focuses its Pane. The composer under the Panes writes to the focused Pane's Timeline, and its prompt shows that Pane's number, said as `Pane 2: #iirc`. The layout is kept on this device and comes back on a reload, without a Pane whose Timeline is gone. On a phone's width only the focused Pane shows, full size, with a row of every Pane's number and name above it to switch with, a Pane named by what it shows, `options`, `search`, or a draft's nick, never the Timeline behind, and so it is while zoomed. The layout is kept as it was, and comes back when the screen is wide again.
- **Marks read where the Owner has seen it.** While the page is visible and focused and a Pane the Owner can see shows a Timeline at its latest, it is marked read up to the last item in view once that has held for a second, so passing through a Timeline marks nothing. Scrolled back, hidden, or in another window, nothing is marked. `/read` marks the open Timeline read up to its latest now, and `/read all` every Timeline. The Daemon keeps the Read Marker, so every Frontend sees its counts clear.
- **Jumps to what calls.** Alt+A, Option+A on a Mac, or `/next-unread`, opens the Timeline with the highest level unread, Highlights first, then Direct Messages, then Messages, the first in the sidebar's order among equals.
- **Searches the Messages of every Timeline,** newest first. `/search <query>`, Mod+Shift+F, Cmd+Shift+F on a Mac, since Mod+F is the browser's own find, or the palette shows the search in the Pane it shows in, or the focused one, in front of its Timeline, with the caret in its field. Enter searches what the field holds: words, as one run of text, or a quoted phrase, narrowed by `from:<nick>`, `in:<#channel|nick>`, `network:<id>`, and `since:` and `until:` a date in the Owner's time zone or a time with its zone, all read by the core, which says in words what it cannot read, as a query too short or `in:` naming Timelines on two Networks. It searches on Enter, not as the Owner types, since a query half typed, such as `from:pe`, would search for something else. A line says how it stands, `Searching…`, `12 found, newest first.`, `Nothing found.`, or that the newest 500 are shown and to narrow it, as a polite status. Each result says its Network and Timeline and when, then its sender in their colour and its text, with what was searched for marked. ArrowDown moves from the field into the results, where the selected one is the listbox's `aria-activedescendant`, kept in the page by the virtualizer's range wherever the results are scrolled, and brought into view as focus enters them. There the arrows, Home, End, and PageUp and PageDown move, the next page read as the Owner nears the end, by keys or by scrolling, and Enter or a press jumps to the result. Escape, from either, shows what the Pane showed before, and the composer takes the focus. What was found is kept, so `/search` alone or Mod+Shift+F shows it again. A merge, an overflow, or a reset on the changes begins the search again from its first page.
- **Jumps to a result where it stands.** Its Timeline opens in the Pane the search is in, or the Pane that has it open already, read around the result, which the view brings to the middle and marks with an accent band that fades after a few seconds, or goes as soon as the Owner scrolls. Live items arrive beyond what it holds, so nothing moves, and "Jump to latest" stays under the log, reading the latest again and following it. A jump marks nothing read: only the view at the latest does.
- **Notifies, when the Owner asks.** `/notify on` asks the browser's permission and, once granted, turns notifications on for this device, and `/notify off` turns them off. A Highlight or a Direct Message said live by someone else, in a Timeline the Owner is not looking at, notifies, titled with its Timeline's name and Network, saying the Message as plain text, cut to 200 characters and isolated, one per Timeline, a newer one replacing it. Choosing it focuses the window and opens that Timeline.
- **Shows a Timeline live,** as terminal lines in columns every line shares: the time, HH:MM in the Owner's time zone, a Message by its sender in their colour, right-aligned, with its formatting and links after it, and our own Messages with how their delivery stands, sending, unconfirmed, not sent, or refused with the Ircd's numeric or code. Activity is dimmer and marked in the senders' column as WeeChat marks it, `-->` for a join, `<--` for a part, a quit, or a kick, and `--` for anything else, its words starting where a Message's text does and wrapping under it, and a Session's line, a refusal, and a Gap's words line up there too. A failed Connection names its Ircd and why, and when its port and TLS look mismatched, the `/set` that tries them the other way, as code. Failed Connections in a row to one Ircd for one reason are one line, the latest with its hint, saying how many and from when to when, `×8, 18:41–18:55`. A Gap is a divider, "not connected from 12:01 to 12:30", with how many when several are one. It opens on the latest, stays at the bottom while live items arrive there, asks for older items as the Owner scrolls near the top, and keeps what is in view still as they go in above it. Scrolled away from the latest, new items wait, and are read as the Owner scrolls back down.
- **Says Messages from a composer** under the Panes, the focused Pane's Timeline's or, with none open, one prompted `iirc` where commands that need no Timeline are typed, a prompt with its name and a textarea that grows to six lines. Enter sends, Shift+Enter starts a new line, Up and Down with no modifier recall what was sent from this Timeline when the caret is on the first or last line, and Escape lets go of an error or a confirmation, or, with neither, closes what stands in front of the focused Pane's Timeline, the options, the search, or a Direct Message not yet begun, and with nothing in front puts away its Lookup card, the focus staying in the composer. Its prompt names what the Pane shows: `options`, `search`, a draft's nick, or the Timeline. Plain text is said to the Channel or Direct Message, `/me` says an action, `/msg <target> <text>` says it to anyone and opens their Direct Message, plain text under a Direct Message not yet begun says it to its nick and opens the Timeline it makes, `/notice` sends a notice, and `//text` says `/text`, all through the core's one command registry. A paste of more than five lines asks first. Each Timeline keeps its own draft. On a Network with no Session, a line under the composer says nothing can be said there, and the composer stays usable for commands that need none. Why something was not said is shown under it in words, tied to the textarea with `aria-describedby`, with a way to send it again when that is safe, or to check again when no answer came. Our own Message appears once, when the Daemon folds it, with how its delivery stands, never drawn ahead of it.
- **Acts on a Network from the composer.** `/topic`, `/kick`, `/mode`, `/op`, `/deop`, `/voice`, `/devoice`, `/ban`, `/unban`, `/invite`, `/away`, `/back`, `/nick`, and `/quote` are the core's commands, giving an `Act`, which goes to `POST /api/networks/{network}/act` as a Say goes to `say`. `/topic` alone puts `/topic` and the topic as the header last read it in the composer to edit. Arguments that depend on the Network, a mode's parameter, a rank, the ban list, are read from the head of the Channel in focus, so `/voice` on a Network whose `PREFIX` has no `v` says so before anything is sent. A refusal `act` answered with is said under the composer, as what was tried. One the Ircd gives later is a line of its own in the Timeline it belongs to, dim and italic, such as "Your topic change for #iirc was refused (482)", never in the Ircd's words. While the Ircd has confirmed Self away on the focused Timeline's Network, the composer's prompt says `(away)`, dim, with the message as its title, from `known`, read again when Self's away changes.
- **Changes Configuration as an option tree** ([ADR 0013](../../docs/adr/0013-configuration-is-an-option-tree.md)). `/network add <id> <host>[/port] [plain] <nick>` adds a Network, enabled, from no Timeline at all. `/disconnect [id]` and `/connect [id]` turn a Network off and on, the focused one unless named, by setting `network.<id>.enabled` as `/set` would, through the same change, and the sidebar shows its connection follow. `/join #a,#b` and `/part [#channel]` change the open Timeline's Network's Channels. `/set` opens the options view, `/set <pattern>` opens it filtered, `/set <option> <value>` sets one to the rest of the line, exactly, `/set <credential option>` alone opens the password prompt for it, and `/unset <option>` returns one to what a new Network starts with, all through the core's registry.
- **Shares the options under `ui` between devices, and lets one override them** ([ADR 0015](../../docs/adr/0015-the-journal-holds-changes-that-belong-to-no-network.md)). `/set ui.time HH:mm:ss` sets one for every device through the Daemon, and every page signed in shows it at once. `/set -device ui.theme light` sets one for this device alone, kept on it and never sent, and `/unset -device ui.theme` goes back to the shared value. The options view lists them after every Network's, each overridden here saying so beside the shared value, and edits, toggles, and unsets them in place: one this device overrides, for this device. `ui.time` shows each line's time as `HH:mm`, with seconds, or not at all, `ui.activity` hides joins, parts, and quits, or all Activity, from a Channel's lines, never from what is counted unread, `ui.theme` names the theme the page draws in, and `ui.colours` draws the colours a Message asks for through the theme or as mIRC gives them.
- **Draws in themes made from terminal palettes.** `/theme`, or `theme` typed into the palette, lists them: `system`, which draws the built-in `dark` or `light` as the device prefers, the built-in Solarized dark and light, Gruvbox dark, Nord, Dracula, and Tokyo Night, and those imported. A theme highlighted in the palette draws the whole page in it at once, and the theme in effect comes back as the highlight moves on, as Escape closes it, or as a choice is made, which draws in it for every device. `/theme <name>` draws in one for every device, and `/theme -device <name>` for this device alone. `/theme import` opens a dialog to paste a theme file's text into, or to choose its file: base16 or base24 YAML, iTerm2's `.itermcolors`, Alacritty's TOML, Kitty's or Ghostty's configuration, or a Windows Terminal scheme. It says the format it read, or why it could not, in words, draws the whole page in the theme as it reads, with a sample of nicks and mIRC's colours, and offers the name the file gives, or its file's, to save it under. Save imports it for every device, and Cancel or Escape draws the theme in effect again. `/theme remove <name>` removes one imported, and a device drawing in it draws `system` instead. Every colour is the core's, derived from the palette and kept readable, so no theme, however ugly, hides text.
- **Shows the options view** in a Pane, the one it shows in already or the focused one, opened by `/set` or the sidebar's Options, until a Timeline is opened again there, by the sidebar or by a `/msg` that opens its Direct Message, or until Escape, from anywhere in it but an edit, a question, or a filter with text, or its `close` button, shows what the Pane showed before, its Timeline, or a Direct Message not yet begun, and gives the composer the focus. Every Network's Configuration and the Credentials held are read each time the options come to show, however they came to, a layout restored with them showing included, and again when `/set` asks while they show. What each Pane shows, and the options' filter, are the core's navigation, in the Timelines' state, and the Panes and the sidebar derive what they show from it. Its filter takes a pattern with `*`, such as `network.libera.*`, or text found anywhere, as the Owner types. Its options are a React Aria `GridList`, each with its name, its value, what a new Network starts with, and what it is, from the Schema's annotations. Arrows move, Enter edits the value in place as its raw text, or turns a toggle over, Enter again sets it and Escape lets it go, and Delete or Backspace asks to unset it, Enter unsetting and Escape keeping it. A Credential option shows its Credential's name and whether the Vault holds it, and Enter opens the password prompt for it, never an inline editor. What a change came to, when it did not go as asked, is said above the options in words, tied to them with `aria-describedby`.
- **Has a command palette,** opened by Mod+K or Mod+Shift+P, Mod being Cmd on a Mac and Ctrl elsewhere, or by `/palette`. It lists what can be done here, in sections: the commands the registry can run, each with its usage, a dim description, and its keys, every Timeline, by name with its kind and Network, and every option, with what it is. Typing filters and ranks them, as the core does, and with nothing typed, what was chosen recently comes first. Arrows move, Enter chooses, and Escape clears what was typed, then closes, with focus back where it was. A command that needs no arguments runs, such as `/set` or `/next`. One that needs them starts in the composer as `/<name> `, focused, when its draft is empty: a draft with text is never replaced, and the composer, focused, says which command was not put in, so the Owner can clear it and choose again. A Timeline opens, with focus back where it was, the composer included. An option starts `/set <name> ` in the composer, in the same way, and a Credential option opens the password prompt. A polite status, hidden from view and present from the moment the palette opens, says how many results there are, `3 results` or `No results`, so a screen reader announces each change.
- **Takes keys from anywhere on the page,** by the core's keymap: Mod+K and Mod+Shift+P open the palette, Alt+ArrowDown and Alt+ArrowUp open the next and previous Timeline in the sidebar's order, wrapping, Alt+A the one calling most, Alt+M shows or hides the members, Mod+, shows the options, Mod+Shift+F shows the search, Alt+Shift and a letter or an arrow change the Panes, and `i`, outside a field, focuses the composer. All but `i` work while typing in the composer, which `i` is typed into. In the sidebar's tree, Alt+Shift and an arrow are the tree's own, which extends or collapses, so the Panes' arrows are taken from the composer or a Pane. A key the keymap does not take goes on as it would.
- **Shows who is here, at the head of a Timeline.** A Channel's header has its name, its topic, formatted as a Message is, with its links, and who set it and when, dim, how many members it has, its modes, and, when Self is not in it, where Self stands in words, such as "not in this Channel — /join to rejoin". A Direct Message's has its partner, and the Network's own Timeline how its connection stands. The head is read from `GET /api/timelines/{id}/channel` as the Channel opens in a Pane, and again, after a moment, when the changes stream carries Activity for it, a Gap of its Network, an overflow naming it, or a reset, for each Channel a Pane shows.
- **Shows each Channel's members, when the Owner asks.** `/members`, Alt+M, Option+M on a Mac, or a header's `members` toggle shows or hides them in every Pane showing a Channel, and the choice is kept for this device. They are a column beside the Timeline in its Pane, and on a phone's width lie over it. Most privileged first, each has their rank's symbol, as `PREFIX` names it, and their nick in its colour, dimmed while away, and a screen reader hears each as `peer, operator, away`. They are read a page at a time from `GET /api/timelines/{id}/members` as the Owner scrolls, and read again from the start, as many as were held, on the same changes as the header. Arrows move, typing a nick finds it, and Enter or a double click opens their Direct Message, as `/query` does. A member chosen offers, right under their nick, in their row, where Tab reaches them, `query`, `whois`, which looks them up as `/whois` does, and `mention`, which starts the composer with their nick and `: `.
- **Opens a Direct Message by nick.** `/query <nick>` opens the Timeline the nick keys on the open Timeline's Network, as the Daemon folds names. When nothing was said with them yet, there is no Timeline to open, and the API makes one only by saying something, so the focused Pane shows a Direct Message not yet begun: a header naming the nick and its Network, with a `close` button, a log saying nothing was said yet, and the composer under it, prompted with the nick, where plain text, `/say`, and `/me` go to the nick. Once the Daemon placed the first Message, the Pane shows the Timeline it made, as a `/msg` placement does, and only that Pane, while it still shows the draft: moving to another Pane or closing it lets the placement go. When the Timeline comes to be listed another way, as when the partner speaks first or another Frontend says something to them, the draft shows it in place, found by the casemapping `/query` read as it made the draft and no other, without taking the focus, and what was typed under the draft, unsent, moves to the Timeline's composer, with its notice, and so does what a Say sent from the draft answers later, such as a failure offering to check or send again. A draft behind the options or the search shows its Timeline once they close, and its text moves then. Escape there or the close button shows what the Pane showed before. When the Daemon cannot say how the Network folds names, nothing opens and the composer says so, and a `/query` the Owner moved on from while it was asked opens nothing.
- **Looks a User up.** `/whois <nick>` and `/whowas <nick>`, from the composer, the palette, or a member chosen in the members' `whois`, show a card in the Pane they were asked from, as a transient panel under its header, over its Timeline, in the header's compact style: "Looking up…", and "Still waiting for the Network's reply…" while the Daemon says `Pending`, then each User described: their nick in its colour, `user@host`, realname, the server and its text, operator, a secure connection, idle humanised, `idle 3m`, signed on, as a date and a time in the Owner's time zone, with seconds when `ui.time` shows them, account, "connects from" and the mask or IP the Ircd says the User actually uses, "modes" and their modes, away message, the Channels with the symbols of their ranks there, each opening its Timeline when one is open, or starting `/join <channel>` in the composer otherwise, and the numerics read for nothing listed dimly as their parameters. A reply cut short says why above what came, and one that found no User says so in words, as not found, refused by the Network with its numeric, or not sent and why. Every text a Network sent is a text node in its own `<bdi>`. Escape, in the card or in the composer with no notice or confirmation to let go of, or its close button, puts it away. A Pane has one card at a time, a new Lookup replacing it, and it shows only while the Pane has open the Timeline it was asked from. Closing the Pane lets its card go, a zoom or a phone's width that hides it keeps it, and signing out lets every card go, so none comes back on signing in again. A signon no `Date` can hold, as a hostile `317` can give, is said to be unavailable, and the rest shows. Tab completes the nick after `/whois ` as it completes any nick.
- **Completes the word at the caret with Tab.** A command after `/` at the start of a line, an option after `/set `, a Channel's name, or a nick, the Channel's members asked of the members route by the word typed, those who spoke latest first, with `: ` after a nick at the start of a line. Tab again moves to the next, Shift+Tab to the one before, and Escape gives back what was typed. With no word at the caret, Tab moves focus on as anywhere. The candidates show dim under the composer, the one in place accented, and a polite status announces each, as the palette's does. The composer stays one `<textarea>`, and a key an input method is composing is left alone.
- **Signs out** with `DELETE /api/sign-in`, which clears the cookie.

## How it is built

| Folder | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| `src/app/` | The shell, the one Effect runtime, the Daemon's client, and how intents are named |
| `src/sign-in/` | The sign-in atoms and commands, and the sign-in screen |
| `src/composer/` | The composer's atoms, what carries out its intents, Tab completion's atoms, and the composer |
| `src/options/` | The options' atoms, what changes a Configuration and holds a Credential, the options under `ui`, followed, overridden on this device, and in effect, and the options view |
| `src/search/` | The search's atoms, what reads a query in the Owner's time zone, and the search view |
| `src/palette/` | The palette's state and the keymap, what the palette lists and what carries out a choice, the palette, and the one key listener |
| `src/timelines/` | The Timelines' atoms, the one changes stream, the layout kept, the sidebar, the Timeline view, its lines, and the view of a Direct Message not yet begun |
| `src/panes/` | The Panes' atoms, the tree drawn with `react-resizable-panels`, one Pane, and the switcher on a compact screen |
| `src/channel/` | Each shown Channel's atoms and its reads, a Pane's header, and its members panel |
| `src/lookup/` | Each Pane's Lookup card's atoms, what carries out a `Look`, and the card |
| `src/reading/` | Marking read, and the page's title |
| `src/notifications/` | The `Notifier` port and its browser adapter, and turning notifications on and off |
| `src/page/` | Whether the Owner can see the page, the one place that listens for it, and what this device keeps of the Owner's choices |
| `src/theme/` | The theme in effect or previewed, drawn as custom properties on the root, choosing, importing, and removing themes, the `ThemeReader` port and the Worker a theme is read in, and the import dialog |
| `src/ui/` | The theme's tokens as the first paint draws them, a button on React Aria Components, a password field that holds no value, and instants shown only where a `Date` can hold them |

- **One Effect runtime.** `Atom.runtime` provides `DaemonClient`, which is `HttpApiClient.make(DaemonApi)` over the browser's `fetch`, with relative URLs, so every request goes to the origin that served the page and carries its cookie. Every atom that reaches the Daemon runs in it, and a test replaces its Layer through `runtime.layer`.
- **State lives in atoms, not components.** The sign-in state is one atom holding the core's `SignIn`, and the commands `check`, `signInWithKey`, and `signOut` are `runtime.fn` atoms that fold each answer in as an event. Components read atoms and run commands. None fetches, and none writes state from an effect, but for a palette row saying it is highlighted, which React Aria reports only as a render prop, and the import dialog closing its import as it unmounts. `src/main.tsx` runs `check` once, before the first render.
- **What can be derived is.** The Timelines' state is one atom holding the core's `Timelines`, and the sidebar and the open Timeline are atoms derived from it. A read refused as `Unauthorized` folds `Refused`, so a sign-in ended elsewhere signs the page out.
- **The core decides what to read, and the runtime reads it.** `sync`, mounted once in `src/main.tsx`, runs while the browser is signed in, from nothing each time it signs in. Whenever the state changes it makes each read `wanted` gives, in a fiber of its own, folding `ReadStarted` first so none is made twice: a window around a position, as a jump reads it, with `around`, and a page of the search with `GET /api/search`, its filters each given only when the query had it. A read the core asked for again, because the last came back too old to use, waits first, longer each time. Each attempt tells the core with `Requesting` just before it asks, after any wait and on every retry, so an answer is judged current from when it was asked, and traffic while the runtime waits cannot keep it stale. A failure is asked again, soon and then up to every 15 s, until it succeeds, except `Unauthorized`, which signs the page out, and `UnknownTimeline` and `UnknownNetwork`, which the core is told. A search refused as `InvalidRequest`, `UnknownTimeline`, or `UnknownNetwork` folds `SearchFailed`, which the view says in words, and is not asked again.
- **One changes stream for the whole page.** Once `followFrom` gives a place, `sync` follows `GET /api/timelines/changes` from it, through the same typed client as every other read, which parses each event with the API's own Schema, and folds each into the core. The place moves with every event and lives in the core's state for as long as the page is open. Whenever the stream ends, fails, or gives nothing, heartbeats included, for 45 s, three heartbeats, it is followed again from the place the core holds, and so it is when opening it gives no answer for as long: the request is given up, which aborts it. A reload starts from nothing and reads everything again, so the place is never stored, since it means nothing without what was read at it. It reads the stream with `fetch` rather than `EventSource`, which would keep its own place apart from the core's, could not tell a refused sign-in from a network failure, and sends a `Last-Event-ID` the core did not choose after a `reset`, which carries none.
- **The core decides what to say, and the runtime says it.** `send`, `confirmSend`, and `takeUp` fold the composer's step, then carry out its intents in order, one submission at a time across the page. A Say, and an Act alike, is made under an intent the runtime names, `web-` and a random UUID, since the core makes no randomness, so the API's recovery holds. On `NetworkStopped`, whose append has settled, it asks `said` under that intent, and the composer offers to send it again only when it was `NotJournaled`. After a request that got no answer, a transport failure or a gateway's `502` or `504`, the request may still be queued or appending, so `said` answering `NotJournaled` leaves it unknown, and the composer offers only to check again, never to send again. A failure stops the intents after it. `Unauthorized` signs the page out.
- **A change of Configuration is made from what it read.** A `Configure` reads `GET /api/networks/{network}/configuration`, applies the core's edit, and, unless that changed nothing, `PUT`s the whole Configuration with `ifSequence`, the sequence it read. ## What it guarantees

- **The Frontend never stores a key** ([ADR 0006](../../docs/adr/0006-proportionate-security.md)). While the Owner types it, the key is held only by the input, as any password field holds it, and nothing in the page keeps it once it has been sent. The input is a plain, uncontrolled `<input type="password">`, so the key is never in React state or props: React Aria's fields keep every keystroke in state, so a secret never goes through one, and React Aria is used where nothing secret is held. On submit, the form is read in one expression and parsed as the API's own `SignInRequest`, straight to `Redacted`. Each field's value is then set to `""`, which also clears the last value React DOM's tracker keeps on every input, controlled or not, and the form is reset, whether the sign-in then succeeds or fails. The sign-in command wipes the `Redacted` once the Daemon has answered, so the argument its atom keeps holds nothing. No key is written to `localStorage`, `sessionStorage`, or a cookie script can read: the only things the page stores are whether this device notifies, once the Owner says `/notify on`, whether it shows the members, once the Owner shows them, its layout, which names Timelines by id, and its overrides of the options under `ui`, `iirc.ui`, once the Owner sets one with `-device`, each an option's name and value.
- **The key field is accessible and a password manager may keep it.** It has a `<label>`, is required, and while a sign-in has failed it is `aria-invalid` and `aria-describedby` the failure's words. Its `autocomplete` is `current-password`, so a password manager can fill in and save the key: that is the Owner's own choice and outside the page's script, and a key held there is no worse than one pasted from a file.
- **Text a Network sent is only ever a text node.** There is no `innerHTML`, and no markup is built from strings. Formatting comes from the core as data, drawn as classes, and a colour a Network asked for as an element's style property, which the policy allows, as is every row's position. Each nick, name, and text sits in a `<bdi>`, so bidirectional formatting in it cannot reorder what is around it. A link is one the core found and parsed, of `http`, `https`, `irc`, or `ircs`, and opens in a new tab with `rel="noopener noreferrer"`.
- **Its icon is two `i`s in block type,** the second dotted in the Highlight colour, drawn on a 32-unit grid so it stays sharp at 16 pixels. `public/favicon.svg` is the tab's icon, and `public/apple-touch-icon.png` is the same at 180 pixels with square corners, which iOS rounds itself, for a phone's home screen.
- **It runs under a strict Content Security Policy.** It loads nothing but its own files, with no inline script or style. The theme's colours are custom properties set on the root's style through the CSSOM, and a run's colours properties on its element's, which the policy allows. React Aria would inject one inline `<style>` for its pressable elements, so `public/pressable.css` carries the same rule and is linked under the id React Aria looks for, which stops it injecting. The palette's Modal injects none on a desktop, setting `overflow` on the root element's style instead, which the policy allows. On iOS WebKit alone, React Aria's scroll locking adds a `<style>` for `overscroll-behavior` with no id to look for, which the policy refuses: the page still works, a violation is reported, and only that rule is lost.
- **An instant out of a `Date`'s range never takes the page down.** An instant a Network gave can be any safe integer, past the ±8.64e15 ms a `Date` holds, where `format` and `toISOString` throw, so every time shown, a line's, a Gap's, a topic's, a search result's, and a signon, goes through `src/ui/dates.ts`, and one out of range shows as unavailable.
- **Every failure is decided from the API's structure.** A typed error, a request that got no answer, or a gateway's `502` or `504`, never the text of a response.

## Development

```sh
bun run dev          # in apps/web: Vite on localhost:5173
```

The dev server proxies `/api` to a Daemon on `127.0.0.1:7667`, so the browser sees one origin. The proxy passes the browser's `Host` and `Origin` through unchanged, so the Daemon sees requests as same-origin, and it streams responses, Server-Sent Events included, without buffering. A Daemon that is not running is answered `502`, which the Frontend reads as no answer.

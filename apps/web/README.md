# @iirc/web

The web frontend: a React 19 view over [`@iirc/frontend`](../../packages/frontend/README.md), its pure core ([ADR 0010](../../docs/adr/0010-the-web-frontend-is-a-pure-core-under-react.md)), built with Vite. It is a Frontend like any other, and reaches the Daemon only through [`@iirc/api`](../../packages/api/README.md), which the lint fence enforces. It is served on the API's own origin, by `iirc daemon --web` ([ADR 0007](../../docs/adr/0007-one-binary-shapes-by-argument.md)).

```sh
bun run build:web                                        # at the root: apps/web/dist
bun run iirc daemon --data-dir ~/.local/share/iirc --web=apps/web/dist
```

## What it does

- **Signs in with a key.** On load it asks whether the browser is signed in, with one authorized read, `GET /api/networks`. Signed out, it asks for a key and gives it to `POST /api/sign-in` once, for a sign-in the browser holds in an `HttpOnly` cookie. Why a sign-in failed is said in words: a wrong key, a request from another origin, the Daemon unable to save its sign-ins, or the Daemon not answering, with a way to try again.
- **Shows the Networks and their Timelines** in a sidebar: a tree of each Network, by id, with how its connection stands, and under it its own Timeline, then its Channels, then its Direct Messages, each by name. Arrows move and expand, Enter or Space opens, typing a name finds it, and choosing a Timeline opens it in the main view.
- **Shows what is new, calmly.** Each sidebar row's name is coloured by the highest level unread in it, from the Daemon's counts: read and Activity dim, Messages as text, Direct Messages and Highlights accented and heavier. Beside it, small numbers: Highlights and Direct Messages accented, other Messages dim, and `999+` past what the Daemon counts. A Network's row sums its Timelines and shows only what calls for the Owner, Highlights and Direct Messages. A row's label says it for a screen reader, such as `#iirc, 3 unread, 1 highlight`, and a Network's adds how its connection stands. The page's title counts the Highlights and Direct Messages unread across every Network, `(3) iirc`. A Highlight in a Timeline is tinted, with a mark at its edge. A Timeline opened with something unread shows a "new" divider before the first item after the Read Marker it opened with, which stays while it stays open, whatever the Daemon's marker does.
- **Marks read where the Owner has seen it.** While the page is visible and focused and the view shows the open Timeline at its latest, it is marked read up to the last item in view once that has held for a second, so passing through a Timeline marks nothing. Scrolled back, hidden, or in another window, nothing is marked. `/read` marks the open Timeline read up to its latest now, and `/read all` every Timeline. The Daemon keeps the Read Marker, so every Frontend sees its counts clear.
- **Jumps to what calls.** Alt+A, Option+A on a Mac, or `/next-unread`, opens the Timeline with the highest level unread, Highlights first, then Direct Messages, then Messages, the first in the sidebar's order among equals.
- **Notifies, when the Owner asks.** `/notify on` asks the browser's permission and, once granted, turns notifications on for this device, and `/notify off` turns them off. A Highlight or a Direct Message said live by someone else, in a Timeline the Owner is not looking at, notifies, titled with its Timeline's name and Network, saying the Message as plain text, cut to 200 characters and isolated, one per Timeline, a newer one replacing it. Choosing it focuses the window and opens that Timeline.
- **Shows a Timeline live,** as terminal lines: the time, HH:MM in the Owner's time zone, a Message by its sender in their colour with its formatting and links, Activity and Sessions dimmer, and our own Messages with how their delivery stands, sending, unconfirmed, not sent, or refused with the Ircd's numeric or code. A Gap is a divider, "not connected from 12:01 to 12:30", with how many when several are one. It opens on the latest, stays at the bottom while live items arrive there, asks for older items as the Owner scrolls near the top, and keeps what is in view still as they go in above it. Scrolled away from the latest, new items wait, and are read as the Owner scrolls back down.
- **Says Messages from a composer** under the main view, the open Timeline's or, with none open, one prompted `iirc` where commands that need no Timeline are typed, a prompt with its name and a textarea that grows to six lines. Enter sends, Shift+Enter starts a new line, Up and Down with no modifier recall what was sent from this Timeline when the caret is on the first or last line, and Escape lets go of an error or a confirmation. Plain text is said to the Channel or Direct Message, `/me` says an action, `/msg <target> <text>` says it to anyone and opens their Direct Message, `/notice` sends a notice, and `//text` says `/text`, all through the core's one command registry. A paste of more than five lines asks first. Each Timeline keeps its own draft. On a Network with no Session, a line under the composer says nothing can be said there, and the composer stays usable for commands that need none. Why something was not said is shown under it in words, tied to the textarea with `aria-describedby`, with a way to send it again when that is safe, or to check again when no answer came. Our own Message appears once, when the Daemon folds it, with how its delivery stands, never drawn ahead of it.
- **Changes Configuration as an option tree** ([ADR 0013](../../docs/adr/0013-configuration-is-an-option-tree.md)). `/network add <id> <host>[/port] [-tls] <nick>` adds a Network, enabled, from no Timeline at all. `/join #a,#b` and `/part [#channel]` change the open Timeline's Network's Channels. `/set` opens the options view, `/set <pattern>` opens it filtered, `/set <option> <value>` sets one to the rest of the line, exactly, `/set <credential option>` alone opens the password prompt for it, and `/unset <option>` returns one to what a new Network starts with, all through the core's registry.
- **Shows the options view** in the main area, opened by `/set` or the sidebar's Options, until a Timeline is opened again, by the sidebar or by a `/msg` that opens its Direct Message. Which of the two the main view shows, and the options' filter, are the core's navigation, in the Timelines' state, and the shell and the sidebar derive what they show from it. Its filter takes a pattern with `*`, such as `network.libera.*`, or text found anywhere, as the Owner types. Its options are a React Aria `GridList`, each with its name, its value, what a new Network starts with, and what it is, from the Schema's annotations. Arrows move, Enter edits the value in place as its raw text, or turns a toggle over, Enter again sets it and Escape lets it go, and Delete or Backspace asks to unset it, Enter unsetting and Escape keeping it. A Credential option shows its Credential's name and whether the Vault holds it, and Enter opens the password prompt for it, never an inline editor. What a change came to, when it did not go as asked, is said above the options in words, tied to them with `aria-describedby`.
- **Has a command palette,** opened by Mod+K or Mod+Shift+P, Mod being Cmd on a Mac and Ctrl elsewhere, or by `/palette`. It lists what can be done here, in sections: the commands the registry can run, each with its usage, a dim description, and its keys, every Timeline, by name with its kind and Network, and every option, with what it is. Typing filters and ranks them, as the core does, and with nothing typed, what was chosen recently comes first. Arrows move, Enter chooses, and Escape clears what was typed, then closes, with focus back where it was. A command that needs no arguments runs, such as `/set` or `/next`. One that needs them starts in the composer as `/<name> `, focused, when its draft is empty: a draft with text is never replaced, and the composer, focused, says which command was not put in, so the Owner can clear it and choose again. A Timeline opens, with focus back where it was, the composer included. An option starts `/set <name> ` in the composer, in the same way, and a Credential option opens the password prompt. A polite status, hidden from view and present from the moment the palette opens, says how many results there are, `3 results` or `No results`, so a screen reader announces each change.
- **Takes keys from anywhere on the page,** by the core's keymap: Mod+K and Mod+Shift+P open the palette, Alt+ArrowDown and Alt+ArrowUp open the next and previous Timeline in the sidebar's order, wrapping, Alt+A the one calling most, Mod+, shows the options, and `i`, outside a field, focuses the composer. All but `i` work while typing in the composer, which `i` is typed into. A key the keymap does not take goes on as it would.
- **Signs out** with `DELETE /api/sign-in`, which clears the cookie.

## How it is built

| Folder | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| `src/app/` | The shell, the one Effect runtime, and the Daemon's client |
| `src/sign-in/` | The sign-in atoms and commands, and the sign-in screen |
| `src/composer/` | The composer's atoms, what carries out its intents, and the composer |
| `src/options/` | The options' atoms, what changes a Configuration and holds a Credential, and the options view |
| `src/palette/` | The palette's state and the keymap, what the palette lists and what carries out a choice, the palette, and the one key listener |
| `src/timelines/` | The Timelines' atoms, the one changes stream, the sidebar, the Timeline view, and its lines |
| `src/reading/` | Marking read, and the page's title |
| `src/notifications/` | The `Notifier` port and its browser adapter, and turning notifications on and off |
| `src/page/` | Whether the Owner can see the page, and the one place that listens for it |
| `src/ui/` | The theme's tokens, a button on React Aria Components, and a password field that holds no value |

- **One Effect runtime.** `Atom.runtime` provides `DaemonClient`, which is `HttpApiClient.make(DaemonApi)` over the browser's `fetch`, with relative URLs, so every request goes to the origin that served the page and carries its cookie. Every atom that reaches the Daemon runs in it, and a test replaces its Layer through `runtime.layer`.
- **State lives in atoms, not components.** The sign-in state is one atom holding the core's `SignIn`, and the commands `check`, `signInWithKey`, and `signOut` are `runtime.fn` atoms that fold each answer in as an event. Components read atoms and run commands. None fetches, and none writes state from an effect. `src/main.tsx` runs `check` once, before the first render.
- **What can be derived is.** The Timelines' state is one atom holding the core's `Timelines`, and the sidebar and the open Timeline are atoms derived from it. A read refused as `Unauthorized` folds `Refused`, so a sign-in ended elsewhere signs the page out.
- **The core decides what to read, and the runtime reads it.** `sync`, mounted once in `src/main.tsx`, runs while the browser is signed in, from nothing each time it signs in. Whenever the state changes it makes each read `wanted` gives, in a fiber of its own, folding `ReadStarted` first so none is made twice. A read the core asked for again, because the last came back too old to use, waits first, longer each time. Each attempt tells the core with `Requesting` just before it asks, after any wait and on every retry, so an answer is judged current from when it was asked, and traffic while the runtime waits cannot keep it stale. A failure is asked again, soon and then up to every 15 s, until it succeeds, except `Unauthorized`, which signs the page out, and `UnknownTimeline` and `UnknownNetwork`, which the core is told.
- **One changes stream for the whole page.** Once `followFrom` gives a place, `sync` follows `GET /api/timelines/changes` from it, through the same typed client as every other read, which parses each event with the API's own Schema, and folds each into the core. The place moves with every event and lives in the core's state for as long as the page is open. Whenever the stream ends, fails, or gives nothing, heartbeats included, for 45 s, three heartbeats, it is followed again from the place the core holds, and so it is when opening it gives no answer for as long: the request is given up, which aborts it. A reload starts from nothing and reads everything again, so the place is never stored, since it means nothing without what was read at it. It reads the stream with `fetch` rather than `EventSource`, which would keep its own place apart from the core's, could not tell a refused sign-in from a network failure, and sends a `Last-Event-ID` the core did not choose after a `reset`, which carries none.
- **The core decides what to say, and the runtime says it.** `send`, `confirmSend`, and `takeUp` fold the composer's step, then carry out its intents in order, one submission at a time across the page. A Say is made under an intent the runtime names, `web-` and a random UUID, since the core makes no randomness, so the API's recovery holds. On `NetworkStopped`, whose append has settled, it asks `said` under that intent, and the composer offers to send it again only when it was `NotJournaled`. After a request that got no answer, a transport failure or a gateway's `502` or `504`, the request may still be queued or appending, so `said` answering `NotJournaled` leaves it unknown, and the composer offers only to check again, never to send again. A failure stops the intents after it. `Unauthorized` signs the page out.
- **A change of Configuration is made from what it read.** A `Configure` reads `GET /api/networks/{network}/configuration`, applies the core's edit, and, unless that changed nothing, `PUT`s the whole Configuration with `ifSequence`, the sequence it read. On `ConfigurationChanged`, another Frontend changed the Network in between, so it reads again and applies the same edit once more, the edit being a pure function of the Configuration, and if that is refused too, the composer says so in words, having changed nothing. `AddNetwork` `PUT`s with `ifSequence=0`, so a Network already configured is never replaced. After each, the Configuration is read again into the options view. One that fails stops the intents after it, and the composer it came from says why.
- **A Credential's value is given only in the password prompt, and goes straight to the Vault.** The prompt, above the composer, is opened by `/set <credential option>` alone or by Enter on the option in the options view. Its field is the sign-in's: a plain, uncontrolled `<input type="password">` with a label, read once on submit as the API's own `CredentialValue`, straight to `Redacted`, then set to `""`, which clears React DOM's tracker too, and the form reset. `hold` reads the Configuration for the Credential the option names, or the one the core derives, `PUT`s the value to `/api/credentials/{name}`, wipes it once the Vault has answered, and points the option at it as any change is made. The prompt closes once it is held, and says why in words, staying open, when it is not. A value typed into the composer after `/set <credential option>` is refused: the textarea's own value is set to `""` as Enter is pressed, the core clears the draft, and `edit` lets go of the text it was last given, which it would otherwise keep as its argument. Until then it was held as typed text, as anything typed into the composer is, and React may keep a past render's props for a while, which is why the prompt is the way to give one.
- **Channels are compared as the Network folds them.** A `Join`, `Part`, or `PartTimeline` first reads `known` for the casemapping an Ircd declared and `CHANTYPES`, which the core's `apply` compares and prefixes names by, and takes neither as known when that read fails. `/part` alone parts by the open Channel Timeline's key, as the Daemon routed it.
- **`/msg` opens where the Daemon put its Message.** A Say with `opens` captures the core's navigation as it is submitted. Once it is `Said`, the runtime waits until the changes stream says the Daemon's projection has folded its step, after a reset under the new projection, and then reads `GET /api/networks/{network}/at/{sequence}` once, which is then the whole answer: the item of its intent, which the Daemon's routing placed in exactly one Timeline, folds `Located` with that Timeline's id and the navigation captured, and no item, as for a Channel's or a refused Say, opens nothing. A read that gets no answer is asked again, soon and then up to every 15 s. Opening another Timeline, or signing out, lets the lookup go at any point, and the core ignores a `Located` from before the Owner opened another. It only reads, so it never says anything. The Frontend never matches a name itself.
- **What became of a Say comes from `say` and the Timeline, never from News.** `say` answers `Refused` with `Offline` or `Unsendable` when the Client refused it as it took it, which the composer says in words, sending nothing after it. Every later outcome is the item's delivery: sending, unconfirmed, confirmed, not sent, or refused with the Ircd's numeric or code.
- **One key listener for the page, and the keymap is the core's.** `Keys`, mounted once with the shell, listens for `keydown` on the document, after every widget's own handlers. It hands each key to `pressed`, in `src/palette/press.ts`, which turns it into the core's plain `KeyEvent`, AltGraph read with `getModifierState`, with whether it was pressed in a field that takes text and whether the palette is open, asks the core's `resolve`, and carries out what it gives, preventing the key's default and stopping it only then. An option's inline editor keeps every key from the grid around it, so it asks `pressed` first, and Mod+K, Mod+Shift+P, and Alt+arrows work there too, leaving the edit as it is. A key a widget already handled, such as the composer's Enter, or one an input method is composing, is left alone. The keymap is an atom holding the core's default, and `Mod` is read once from the browser's user agent.
- **The palette is React Aria's Autocomplete over a Menu, in a Modal's Dialog,** as React Aria's own command palette is built. Its query and whether it is open are the core's palette state, in an atom, and what it lists is derived from the Timelines, the options, the keymap, and that state, ranked by the core, so the Autocomplete filters nothing itself. Opening it reads the options, as opening the options view does, so it lists every option of every Network. A choice is remembered for the page's life and carried out as the core says.
- **The core decides when marking read is due, and the runtime waits, then marks.** `marking`, mounted once in `src/main.tsx`, runs while the browser is signed in. It watches what the core's `markDue` says of the Timelines and the page. Once a mark is due, it waits one second, `settle`, and lets it go if in that time the open Timeline changed or stopped being seen. Then it `POST`s `/api/networks/{network}/read` with what is due then, which is up to the last item the view said is in view, and does not mark again until what is due changes, as it does once the Daemon's Read Marker comes back on the changes, or a new item arrives. A mark that gets no answer is asked again, soon and then up to every 15 s, and `Unauthorized` signs the page out. `/read` and `/read all` are `MarkRead` intents, carried out in turn with the composer's others, and a failure is said under the composer.
- **Whether the Owner can see the page is kept in one place.** `watchPage`, called once in `src/main.tsx`, listens for the document's `visibilitychange` and the window's `focus` and `blur`, and keeps `pageState`, `{ visible, focused }`, which the core reads. It also writes `document.title` from the derived title, as it changes. Nothing else listens to the page for this, and a test sets `pageState` itself.
- **Notifications go through a port.** `Notifier` is an Effect service with the browser's Notification API as its default and a test's own in `src/testing/notifier.ts`. Each `changed` event the stream folds is given to the core's `notificationsOf` with the state before and after it, and what it decides is shown when the Owner turned notifications on for this device and the browser grants them, each with its Timeline's tag, so a newer one replaces the last. The choice is held in memory for the page, read from `localStorage`, as `iirc.notify`, once when it can be, and written back as best it can: storage can be refused, as in a private window, and then `/notify on` or `off` still holds for the page, and the composer says it holds for this page only. It lives there rather than in the Configuration because options under `ui`, which every device would share, do not exist yet, and whether a device notifies is that device's own choice.
- **Intents from the palette or a key are carried out as the composer's are.** Those carried out in the page, opening a Timeline, the palette, the options, or the password prompt, and focusing the composer, are done at once, so moving between Timelines never waits behind a Say in flight. The rest wait their turn with every other submission, and the composer under the main view says why one failed. Focusing the composer starts it with text only when its draft is empty, as the core's `started` decides, and puts the caret at the end. The composer is one element under every Timeline, so focus stays in it as Timelines open, and the Modal's focus scope gives focus back to it after a choice.
- **Styles are CSS Modules over the theme.** `src/ui/theme.css` holds every colour, size, and font as a CSS variable, dark and light by `prefers-color-scheme`, and no module names a colour of its own. What is unread takes its colour from `--color-level-none`, `--color-level-activity`, `--color-level-message`, `--color-level-direct`, and `--color-level-highlight`, and a Highlight's line its tint from `--color-highlight-surface`. Nicks take their colour from one of 16 slots, `--nick-0` to `--nick-15`, and mIRC's colours 0 to 15 are `--irc-0` to `--irc-15`.
- **The Timeline view is TanStack Virtual's,** with only the rows near the view in the page. Its rows sit at the bottom when they are fewer than the view holds, as in a chat. Where the view stands is kept in refs as the Owner scrolls, never in state: at the bottom with nothing beyond, it stays there as rows arrive or are measured, and when rows are added above or the oldest dropped, the row in view is put back where it was. Once it has laid out, and as the Owner scrolls, it tells the core whether the Owner is at the latest, with the last item in view, the last item row that overlaps the view, none when the view has no height or shows only a Gap, the divider, or the start, so the core keeps live items only while they are wanted and marks read only what was shown. Opening the Timeline already open leaves the view where it was. A row above the view that changes height is kept still by the virtualizer itself. The start of a Timeline is a row of its own, so it is anchored as every row is. Each module's class names are declared in a `.module.d.css.ts` beside it, so a class that does not exist fails to compile.

## What it guarantees

- **The Frontend never stores a key** ([ADR 0006](../../docs/adr/0006-proportionate-security.md)). While the Owner types it, the key is held only by the input, as any password field holds it, and nothing in the page keeps it once it has been sent. The input is a plain, uncontrolled `<input type="password">`, so the key is never in React state or props: React Aria's fields keep every keystroke in state, so a secret never goes through one, and React Aria is used where nothing secret is held. On submit, the form is read in one expression and parsed as the API's own `SignInRequest`, straight to `Redacted`. Each field's value is then set to `""`, which also clears the last value React DOM's tracker keeps on every input, controlled or not, and the form is reset, whether the sign-in then succeeds or fails. The sign-in command wipes the `Redacted` once the Daemon has answered, so the argument its atom keeps holds nothing. No key is written to `localStorage`, `sessionStorage`, or a cookie script can read: the only thing the page stores is whether this device notifies, once the Owner says `/notify on`.
- **The key field is accessible and a password manager may keep it.** It has a `<label>`, is required, and while a sign-in has failed it is `aria-invalid` and `aria-describedby` the failure's words. Its `autocomplete` is `current-password`, so a password manager can fill in and save the key: that is the Owner's own choice and outside the page's script, and a key held there is no worse than one pasted from a file.
- **Text a Network sent is only ever a text node.** There is no `innerHTML`, and no markup is built from strings. Formatting comes from the core as data, drawn as classes, and a colour a Network asked for as an element's style property, which the policy allows, as is every row's position. Each nick, name, and text sits in a `<bdi>`, so bidirectional formatting in it cannot reorder what is around it. A link is one the core found and parsed, of `http`, `https`, `irc`, or `ircs`, and opens in a new tab with `rel="noopener noreferrer"`.
- **It runs under a strict Content Security Policy.** It loads nothing but its own files, with no inline script or style. React Aria would inject one inline `<style>` for its pressable elements, so `public/pressable.css` carries the same rule and is linked under the id React Aria looks for, which stops it injecting. The palette's Modal injects none on a desktop, setting `overflow` on the root element's style instead, which the policy allows. On iOS WebKit alone, React Aria's scroll locking adds a `<style>` for `overscroll-behavior` with no id to look for, which the policy refuses: the page still works, a violation is reported, and only that rule is lost.
- **Every failure is decided from the API's structure.** A typed error, a request that got no answer, or a gateway's `502` or `504`, never the text of a response.

## Development

```sh
bun run dev          # in apps/web: Vite on localhost:5173
```

The dev server proxies `/api` to a Daemon on `127.0.0.1:7667`, so the browser sees one origin. The proxy passes the browser's `Host` and `Origin` through unchanged, so the Daemon sees requests as same-origin, and it streams responses, Server-Sent Events included, without buffering. A Daemon that is not running is answered `502`, which the Frontend reads as no answer.

## Tests

`bun run test` runs ten files, sharing the fake Daemon in `src/testing/fake-daemon.ts`, which also serves marking read, which it keeps, or answers with nothing as a test asks, the Network list, `known`, a Network's Timelines, windows, `say`, `said`, the items a step placed, the changes stream as Server-Sent Events, which a test sends events on and ends, a Network's Configuration, read and `PUT` with its precondition, where a test can have another Frontend change it first, and the Credentials, held with their values as a Vault would. Its `say` answers as a test asks: said, refused as `Offline`, `NetworkStopped` journaled or not, `UnknownNetwork`, or no answer.

`src/sign-in/sign-in-screen.test.tsx` renders the Frontend in happy-dom and types a key as a keystroke does, the value set beneath React's tracking and an `input` event after. It walks everything React keeps from its root, state and props, and everything the atom registry holds, for the key. It also reads React DOM's value tracker on the input directly, since it keeps the last value typed in a closure no walk reaches. It covers a key held by neither while typed, and, after a failed sign-in, the field empty, its tracker holding `""`, neither holding the key, and the failure shown and tied to the field, and after a sign-in, the same. Put React Aria's `TextField` in place of the field and the first fails, since it holds the key while typed. Drop the explicit clear before the reset and both fail, since the tracker keeps the key.

`src/sign-in/atoms.test.ts`: the atoms and commands against a fake Daemon at the `fetch` level, through the real `DaemonClient` and the API's own decoding. It covers a browser with no sign-in found signed out, signing in with the key and reading the Networks into the sidebar, the key wiped once answered and sent once, a wrong key said as one and wiped too, the Daemon unable to save the sign-in, signing out and staying signed in when that fails, a refused read signing the page out, and a Daemon that does not answer, directly or through a gateway, checked again once it does.

`src/timelines/atoms.test.ts`: the runtime against the fake, through the real `DaemonClient` and the API's decoding, with its waits made short. It covers reading the Networks, their Timelines, and their connections, then following the changes after the `through` read; opening a Timeline on its latest and a live item arriving; following again from the last place given when the stream ends; the Network list taken as current from when it was asked while overflows come during each wait to ask again and none while it is asked, which the core's property cannot show since it drains the stream before it settles; opening the stream given up when no answer begins, by a request the fake holds until it is aborted, and followed again from the core's place; a heartbeat changing nothing; an overflow naming the open window, which is read again, and a reset, which reads everything again and follows from its place; and older items read `before` the first item held, as the Owner scrolls up.

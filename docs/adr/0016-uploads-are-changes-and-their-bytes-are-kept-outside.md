# Uploads are Changes, and their bytes are kept outside the Journal

An Upload is recorded in the Journal as Changes that belong to no Network, so every Frontend sees the same Uploads, the list survives a restart, and it can be read at any past sequence. These are a second kind of Change. The first, [ADR 0015](0015-the-journal-holds-changes-that-belong-to-no-network.md)'s, is the whole value of one option, so applying it twice is applying it once and the latest wins. An Upload's Changes are records keyed by its id instead: `Uploaded` gives the id, its names, the type read from the bytes, the size, and the hash of the stored bytes, while `Renamed` and `Deleted` each refer to an earlier `Uploaded`. Applying an `Uploaded` twice is still applying it once, but no Upload Change replaces another, and the Uploads are the fold of all of them. Folding them into the options would have made each Upload an option with no default and no meaning apart from its history.

The bytes themselves are kept in a store, by their hash, and never in the Journal. This is not an exception to [ADR 0002](0002-the-journal-holds-bytes.md). That decision keeps the bytes that crossed a Connection, because a parsed form would freeze today's parser into history. An Upload's bytes are not a Line and are never parsed into anything the Daemon derives from. They are content-addressed, so the `Uploaded` Change already names them exactly, and the record has to outlive them: a deleted or expired Upload stays recorded after its bytes are released, and an Upload whose bucket was lost is still one the Owner made.

## Consequences

- Records outlive bytes. A `Deleted` Upload stays in the projection, marked deleted, while its hold on the bytes is released. Bytes are deleted from the store once no live Upload refers to their hash.
- Whether an Upload's bytes are missing is a fact about the store, not a Change, so it is never journaled. The Daemon learns it from a store read and holds it beside the projection, and learns it again after a restart.
- A Change may be made by the Daemon on the Owner's behalf, carrying out a standing option the Owner set. An Upload that passes `uploads.expireAfter` gets a `Deleted` Change marked expired, journaled by the Daemon. It is still a Change: the Owner chose it when they set the option, and recording it is what keeps the list derivable rather than computed from the clock at each read.
- The Owner's options grow beyond `ui` to further roots, `store` and `uploads` first, in the same option tree as [ADR 0013](0013-configuration-is-an-option-tree.md). Their Schemas live in the contract, and the Daemon folds and serves them as it does `ui`. They are the Owner's, not a device's, so a device cannot override them.
- Nothing about where an Upload was said is journaled. It is read from the Messages already in the Timelines.
